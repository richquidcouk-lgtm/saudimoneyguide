"What does it cost to live in Saudi Arabia?" doesn't have one honest answer — a single professional in a shared apartment in a smaller city and a family of four renting a villa in Riyadh have almost nothing in common financially. What's actually useful is understanding which categories drive most of the variation, so you can build a realistic budget for your own situation instead of anchoring on a generic number.

## The Categories That Actually Matter

### Housing

Usually the single biggest line item, and the one with the widest range. It's driven by:
- **City** — Riyadh and Jeddah are generally the most expensive major cities; smaller cities and regional centers are typically cheaper.
- **Neighborhood** — costs vary sharply within the same city depending on proximity to business districts, international schools, and compound-style expat housing versus local neighborhoods.
- **Villa vs apartment, compound vs standalone** — compound housing (common among some expat families, often including shared amenities and security) commands a premium over standalone apartments.
- **Furnished vs unfurnished** — furnished units cost more monthly but avoid a large upfront furnishing cost.

<Callout type="tip">
  Rent is often paid annually or in a small number of large installments rather than monthly, which is a genuine cash-flow planning issue distinct from the total annual cost. Budget for the lump sum, not just the "monthly equivalent."
</Callout>

### Transportation

- Owning a car (see our [car finance](/guides/car-finance-saudi-arabia) and [car insurance](/guides/car-insurance-saudi-arabia) guides) is the norm for most residents outside dense central areas, and fuel costs in Saudi Arabia are generally low by international standards.
- Ride-hailing apps are widely available and commonly used as a car-free alternative, particularly by newer arrivals before they've arranged their own vehicle.
- Public transport infrastructure varies significantly by city and is expanding, particularly in Riyadh.

### Schooling (If You Have Children)

For expat families, international school fees are frequently one of the largest annual expenses — often larger than rent — and vary enormously by school reputation, curriculum (British, American, IB, etc.), and city. If you're negotiating an employment package, school fee allowances are worth treating as a serious, separate negotiation item, not an afterthought.

### Health Insurance

Covered in depth in our [health insurance guide](/guides/health-insurance-expats-saudi-arabia) — your own coverage is typically employer-funded, but dependents' coverage is frequently your own cost and genuinely varies by age and health history.

### Groceries and Everyday Spending

Generally reasonable relative to housing and schooling as a share of a typical budget, though imported goods and Western-brand groceries cost more than local equivalents — a household that shops primarily local markets and produce will spend meaningfully less than one relying heavily on imported products.

### VAT

Saudi Arabia's standard VAT rate is 15%, applied to most goods and services — factor this into any price comparison you're doing against a country with a different tax structure. Use our [VAT calculator](/tools/vat-calculator) to quickly add or strip VAT from a price.

## Riyadh and Jeddah vs Smaller Cities

As a general pattern (not a precise ranking that holds in every case): Riyadh, as the capital and a major business hub, and Jeddah, as a major commercial and coastal city, tend to have the highest housing and lifestyle costs. Smaller cities and regional centers are typically more affordable across most categories, though this can trade off against access to certain international schools, specific employers, or specific amenities concentrated in the larger cities.

## Building Your Own Realistic Budget

1. **Start with your actual take-home pay** — use our [take-home salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator) if GOSI deductions apply to you.
2. **List fixed costs first**: rent (as an annualized figure, then divided to a monthly cash-flow need), school fees if applicable, insurance, and any existing debt or BNPL obligations.
3. **Estimate variable costs realistically**: groceries, transport, discretionary spending — based on your actual habits, not an idealized minimal budget.
4. **Set a savings target** — see our [savings goal calculator](/tools/savings-goal-calculator) — rather than treating savings as whatever happens to be left over.
5. **Revisit annually**, especially around your rent renewal date, since housing costs are usually the biggest source of year-to-year change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Saudi Arabia expensive compared to other Gulf countries?**
It varies by category and city — housing in central Riyadh or Jeddah can be comparable to other major Gulf cities, while overall living costs outside the largest cities are often more moderate. There's no single honest number; it depends heavily on your specific city, housing choice, and lifestyle.

**Do I pay income tax in Saudi Arabia?**
Saudi Arabia does not levy personal income tax on employment salaries, which is a meaningful structural difference from many other countries when comparing take-home pay.

**Is it cheaper to live in a compound or a standalone apartment?**
Standalone apartments are typically cheaper, but compounds often bundle amenities, security, and community features that some expat families specifically value enough to pay the premium for — it's a preference trade-off as much as a pure cost one.

**How much should I budget for setting up when I first arrive?**
Beyond the ongoing monthly costs, budget for one-off setup costs: potentially a large upfront rent payment, furnishing if your unit is unfurnished, and initial transportation arrangements before you have a car — this initial period is often more expensive than an average month once you're settled.

## Summary

Cost of living in Saudi Arabia is driven overwhelmingly by housing city/neighborhood choice and, for families, school fees — far more than by everyday grocery or transport spending. Build your budget from your own actual fixed costs and realistic variable spending rather than a generic "cost of living in Saudi Arabia" number, since the honest range between a single professional and a family with school-age children is enormous.
