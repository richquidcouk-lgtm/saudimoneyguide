Health insurance in Saudi Arabia isn't optional for expats — it's a legal requirement tied directly to your residency. Understanding what your coverage actually includes, before you need it, is one of the more consequential pieces of financial admin to get right early.

## Health Insurance Is Mandatory

Saudi Arabia requires health insurance for all expatriate residents and their dependents, regulated under the Council of Cooperative Health Insurance (CCHI) framework. In practice, this means:

- **Your employer is responsible for providing you with health insurance** as a condition of your employment and Iqama.
- **Eligible family members** are also covered by the private-sector employer's insurance obligation. CHI lists wives, sons up to age 25, and unmarried unemployed daughters, with specific rules for a female employee's husband. Do not assume you must privately fund mandatory family coverage. [Check CHI's family-coverage guidance](https://www.chi.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/chi-bulletin/ask%20Daman/25.pdf).
- **Coverage tiers vary significantly** — employer-provided plans range from a basic mandatory minimum to considerably more comprehensive coverage, depending on your employer, seniority, and negotiated benefits.

<Callout type="info">
  "I have health insurance" and "I have health insurance that actually covers what I need" are different things. The mandatory minimum coverage is exactly that — a minimum. It's worth reading your policy's actual coverage limits, not just confirming a policy exists.
</Callout>

## What to Actually Check in Your Policy

- **Annual coverage limit** — check the maximum annual benefit and any treatment-specific limits in the policy schedule.
- **Network of hospitals and clinics** — whether your plan covers the facilities you'd actually want to use, and whether you need pre-approval for certain treatments or specialists.
- **Maternity coverage** — check the applicable benefits, limits, network and approval requirements in the policy schedule.
- **Pre-existing condition handling** — how the policy treats conditions you had before the coverage started.
- **Dental and optical** — check the included benefits and any sublimits or co-payments; do not assume these categories are entirely excluded.
- **Repatriation and emergency evacuation** — whether serious emergencies requiring transport are covered.

## If Your Employer's Coverage Feels Thin

Some employees choose to add a supplementary/top-up private health insurance policy on top of the mandatory employer coverage, particularly if:

- Your employer's plan has a low annual limit
- You want access to a wider network of private hospitals
- You want optional benefits beyond the mandatory coverage for your eligible family members

This is a genuine extra monthly cost, so weigh it against your actual risk tolerance and family situation rather than defaulting to either extreme.

## For Dependents

For private-sector employees, first confirm that every eligible family member is registered under the employer's mandatory coverage. If someone is missing, ask HR to correct it and consult CHI's complaint route. Optional upgrades and family members outside the mandatory eligibility rules may require separate arrangements; establish eligibility before budgeting for a private policy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What happens if my health insurance lapses?**
Continuous mandatory health insurance is tied to residency compliance — a lapse can create real complications with your Iqama status, not just a gap in medical coverage. Don't let a policy lapse between jobs or during a transition without arranging replacement coverage.

**Can I choose my own insurer, or does my employer decide?**
Your employer typically arranges and pays for your primary mandatory coverage with an insurer of their choice. You can usually arrange separate supplementary coverage independently if you want more than the employer plan provides.

**Does health insurance cost come out of my salary?**
Mandatory coverage for a private-sector employee and eligible family members is the employer's obligation. Optional upgrades are a separate matter. Ask HR for the policy and confirm any disputed deduction or eligibility issue with CHI.

**Is health insurance different for Saudi citizens?**
Saudi citizens may also use public healthcare, but private-sector employers must insure both Saudi and non-Saudi employees and their eligible family members under the applicable rules.

## Summary

For private-sector employees, mandatory health insurance covers the worker and eligible family members through the employer. Confirm registration, read the benefits and network, and separate optional upgrades from the employer's mandatory obligations.
