Saudi Arabia has one of the largest remittance outflows in the world — millions of residents send money home every month. The mechanics are simple once you understand one rule: every legal channel runs through a SAMA-licensed institution, and that single fact shapes almost everything else about how to do this safely and cheaply.

<Callout type="warning">
  Only use banks and SAMA-licensed money exchange centers (Category A centers, specifically, are the ones authorized to issue and accept remittances). Informal, unlicensed transfer arrangements — sometimes called Hawala — are illegal in Saudi Arabia and carry real legal risk for both sender and recipient, on top of offering you zero protection if something goes wrong.
</Callout>

Whichever you choose, you'll need a unified reference number tied to your national ID or Iqama to send a transfer — this isn't optional paperwork, it's how SAMA's anti-money-laundering framework tracks legitimate transfers.

## What actually determines the real cost

The advertised "fee" is often not the real cost. Two things combine to determine what actually arrives on the other end:

The exchange rate margin is the gap between the real mid-market exchange rate and the rate the provider actually gives you — it's frequently larger than the flat fee, but far less visible, since it's baked into the number rather than shown as a line item. A "zero fee" transfer with a bad exchange rate can cost more overall than a transfer with a visible fee and a fair rate.

## How to actually compare providers

1. **Check the exact amount that arrives**, not the fee charged. Enter the same send amount into two providers' calculators and compare the final received amount in the destination currency directly.
2. **Check delivery speed**, if timing matters — bank wires can take longer than dedicated remittance apps for some corridors.
3. **Check where the money actually lands** — straight to a bank account, cash pickup, or mobile wallet — since not every option is available to every recipient.
4. **Confirm the provider is actually SAMA-licensed** before sending anything meaningful through it, especially for a newer app you haven't used before.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a limit on how much I can send home from Saudi Arabia?**
Licensed providers apply their own limits and due-diligence checks, particularly for larger transfers, as part of SAMA's anti-money-laundering requirements — check the specific provider's limits and documentation requirements directly rather than assuming a fixed universal cap.

**Can I use an informal transfer arrangement if it's cheaper?**
No — unlicensed transfer channels (Hawala) are illegal in Saudi Arabia, regardless of how the price compares to a licensed option. The legal and financial risk (no recourse if funds don't arrive, potential legal consequences) makes this false economy.

**Do I need a Saudi bank account to send a remittance?**
Not necessarily — licensed money exchange centers can accept cash or card payments for a transfer without you needing an account there, though a bank's own transfer service naturally requires you to bank with them.

**Does my nationality affect which remittance options are available to me?**
The channels themselves (banks, licensed exchangers, licensed apps) are available regardless of nationality, but specific corridors, partner networks in the destination country, and documentation requirements can vary — check the specific provider for your destination country.

## Summary

Every legal way to send money out of Saudi Arabia runs through a SAMA-licensed bank, exchange center, or fintech app — that's non-negotiable. Within those legal options, the real cost comparison is the exchange rate margin plus the flat fee together, not the advertised fee alone, so always compare the actual amount that lands on the other end before choosing a provider.
