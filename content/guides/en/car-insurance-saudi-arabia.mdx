Car insurance is mandatory in Saudi Arabia — you legally cannot register or renew a vehicle without valid coverage. The real decision most drivers face isn't whether to insure, but which level of coverage actually makes sense for their car and situation.

## Third-Party Insurance: The Legal Minimum

Third-party liability insurance is the mandatory minimum. It covers damage or injury **you cause to other people and their property** — it does not cover damage to your own car, regardless of who's at fault.

- Required by law to register or renew any vehicle.
- Generally the cheapest available option.
- Makes sense for older, lower-value cars where repairing or replacing your own vehicle out of pocket is a manageable risk.

## Comprehensive Insurance: Covers Your Own Car Too

Comprehensive insurance includes third-party liability **plus** cover for your own vehicle — against accidents, theft, fire, and (depending on the policy) other named risks.

- Costs more than third-party, since the insurer is taking on more risk.
- Makes more sense for newer or higher-value cars, especially if you're still financing it (see our [car finance guide](/guides/car-finance-saudi-arabia)) — many lenders actually require comprehensive coverage as a condition of financing, since the car is their collateral.
- Often includes extras like roadside assistance, a replacement car during repairs, or agency (dealer) repair options, depending on the policy tier.

<Callout type="tip">
  If your car is still being financed, check your finance contract before assuming you have a choice — many banks and finance companies require comprehensive coverage for the life of the financing, not just third-party.
</Callout>

## What Actually Moves Your Premium

- **Your driving record** — a history of claims or violations typically increases your premium.
- **The car's value, make, and model** — more expensive cars, and models that are statistically more likely to be stolen or in costly accidents, cost more to insure.
- **Your age and driving experience** — newer drivers commonly pay more.
- **No-claims history** — many insurers offer a no-claims discount that builds up over consecutive claim-free years, and resets or reduces if you make a claim.
- **Coverage add-ons** — agency repair, a higher coverage limit, or lower deductible/excess all increase the premium.

## How to Actually Compare Quotes

1. **Compare like-for-like coverage levels** — a cheap comprehensive quote might have a much higher deductible or lower coverage limit than a pricier one. The headline price alone isn't a fair comparison.
2. **Check the repair network** — whether the policy uses agency (dealer) repair or third-party workshops affects both cost and the quality of parts/service you'll get if you claim.
3. **Understand the deductible/excess** — the amount you pay out of pocket before the insurer covers the rest. A lower premium often comes with a higher deductible.
4. **Ask about the no-claims discount transfer** — if you're switching insurers, confirm whether your no-claims history carries over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is third-party insurance enough if my car is old?**
For a car whose repair or replacement cost is low relative to what comprehensive coverage would cost you annually, third-party can be a reasonable, deliberate choice — as long as you're comfortable covering your own repair costs out of pocket if something happens.

**Does insurance cover the other driver if I lend them my car?**
This depends heavily on the specific policy — some extend cover to permitted drivers, others don't, or only under certain conditions. Check your policy's named-driver terms before assuming coverage extends to someone else driving your car.

**What happens if I don't renew my insurance on time?**
A lapse typically means you can't legally drive the car, and you may face fines or complications at vehicle registration renewal. Set a reminder well before your policy expires.

**Can expats buy car insurance in Saudi Arabia?**
Yes — car insurance is tied to the vehicle and driver, not nationality, though you'll need a valid Saudi driving license and the standard documentation (Iqama, vehicle registration) to purchase a policy.

## Summary

Third-party insurance is the legal minimum and covers damage you cause to others; comprehensive adds cover for your own car and is often required if you're still financing the vehicle. Compare quotes on actual coverage level and deductible, not just the headline premium, and check whether your finance contract requires comprehensive coverage before assuming you have a free choice.
