VAT touches almost every purchase you make in Saudi Arabia, which makes it worth actually understanding rather than just accepting as a line on a receipt. Here's what it is, what it applies to, and how the rate got to where it is today.

## What VAT Is

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax added at each stage of the supply chain, ultimately paid by the end consumer — you see it added to the price when you buy goods or services. Businesses collect it on your behalf and remit it to the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA).

## The Rate: 15%

Saudi Arabia introduced VAT on 1 January 2018 at a standard rate of 5%. The rate was raised to **15%** on 1 July 2020, as part of a set of fiscal measures. That 15% standard rate is what applies to most goods and services today.

## What's Zero-Rated or Exempt

Not everything carries the standard 15% rate. Broadly, some categories receive different treatment — this commonly includes certain exports, some financial services, and specific categories of healthcare and education-related supplies, among others defined in VAT regulations. The exact scope of exemptions and zero-rating is set out in ZATCA's regulations and can change, so for anything commercially significant (running a business, a major purchase), confirm the current treatment directly with ZATCA's published guidance rather than assuming based on general categories.

## How VAT Shows Up in Everyday Life

- **Retail prices** are generally displayed inclusive of VAT for consumer purchases, so the price you see at checkout is usually the price you pay — no surprise addition at the till for typical retail shopping.
- **B2B invoices and larger purchases** (property, cars, professional services) often itemize VAT separately, which matters if you're comparing a "plus VAT" quote against a VAT-inclusive one.
- **Property transactions** have their own specific tax treatment that has evolved over time (including a separate Real Estate Transaction Tax framework distinct from standard VAT for many property sales) — if you're buying property, this is worth confirming specifically rather than assuming standard VAT rules apply, since property tax treatment is a distinct area from general VAT.

## Calculating VAT Yourself

**Adding VAT to a net price:**
VAT amount = net price × 15%
Total price = net price × 1.15

**Removing VAT from a gross (VAT-inclusive) price:**
Net price = gross price ÷ 1.15
VAT amount = gross price − net price

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do tourists get a VAT refund when leaving Saudi Arabia?**
VAT refund schemes for visitors exist in some countries but the availability and mechanics specifically for Saudi Arabia can change — check ZATCA's current guidance or ask at point of purchase if this matters for a specific trip, rather than assuming a refund is automatically available.

**Is VAT the same as income tax?**
No — VAT is a consumption tax on purchases, entirely separate from income tax. Saudi Arabia does not levy personal income tax on employment salaries, which is a different and unrelated fact from the VAT rate on purchases.

**Does VAT apply to online purchases and imports?**
Generally yes, imported goods and many online purchases are subject to VAT under the same framework, though the exact mechanics (who collects it, at what point) can differ from a simple in-store retail purchase.

**Why did the rate change from 5% to 15%?**
The rate increase in July 2020 was part of a broader set of fiscal measures. For the purposes of your own budgeting today, what matters is that 15% is the current standard rate — historical context aside.

## Summary

Saudi Arabia's standard VAT rate is 15%, applied to most goods and services, with some specific categories treated differently under ZATCA's regulations. Retail prices are usually shown VAT-inclusive, so what you see is what you pay — but for larger purchases and business transactions, always clarify whether a quoted price is VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive before committing.
