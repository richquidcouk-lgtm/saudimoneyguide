## Why this matters practically

Knowing which body governs which product tells you where to actually go when something's wrong, instead of guessing:

- A bank refused to open your account or a card fee looks wrong → this is SAMA's regulatory territory; the bank's own complaints process is the first stop, SAMA is the escalation path.
- An investment app or broker is behaving oddly → check it's CMA-authorized before you do anything else.
- A VAT charge looks incorrect on a receipt → that's a ZATCA question, not a bank question.
- Your GOSI contributions look wrong on a payslip → that's between you, your employer, and GOSI directly — see our [full GOSI guide](/guides/gosi-retirement-saudi-arabia).
- A credit application was rejected and you want to know why → pull your report from SIMAH — see our [SIMAH guide](/guides/simah-credit-score) for exactly how.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is SAMA the same as a bank?**
No — SAMA is the regulator that licenses and supervises banks, it isn't a retail bank you can open an account with.

**Do I deal with GOSI directly, or through my employer?**
Your employer registers you and remits contributions; you interact with GOSI directly only to check your own record, which you can do via their online platform.

**Which one of these actually affects my day-to-day banking the most?**
SAMA, by far — it sets the rules for the bank accounts, cards, and consumer finance products you use every day.

## Summary

Five bodies, five different jobs: SAMA for banks and lending, CMA for investing, ZATCA for tax, GOSI for social insurance, SIMAH for your credit file. Most personal-finance questions map cleanly to exactly one of them — knowing which one saves you from emailing the wrong place.
