A claim marked “rejected” tells you the outcome, but not necessarily what to do next. Start by finding the decision reason and the policy section the insurer relied on. A missing document requires a different response from a disagreement about coverage.

## Build a one-page claim record

Record the policy and claim references, service or incident date, amount claimed, decision date and written reason. Keep copies of the policy schedule, relevant terms, invoices and correspondence. Share these only through appropriate official channels; avoid posting medical reports or identity documents in public discussions.

## Match the evidence to the reason

| Stated issue | A useful next check |
| --- | --- |
| Missing information | Ask which exact document or field is missing |
| Coverage dispute | Request the relevant clause and explanation |
| Amount differs from the invoice | Ask for the calculation and deductions |
| Policy or date mismatch | Compare the incident date and policy record |

This table is a way to organize questions, not a promise that a decision will be reversed. For a health issue, do not delay necessary clinical advice while preparing an administrative complaint. For motor insurance, keep factual incident and assessment records together.

## Request an internal review

Write a concise timeline, explain the point you dispute and state what response you seek. For example: “Please identify the policy clause used to reject claim X and confirm whether the attached invoice resolves the missing-document issue.” Ask for a reference number and retain the response.

Avoid repeatedly sending an entire file without identifying what changed. A short explanation attached to the relevant new evidence is easier to reconcile than several inconsistent versions of the claim.

## Use the current official route

The [Insurance Authority’s complaint service](https://www.ia.gov.sa/en/eservices-ia/service-details/Complaint-against-an-insurance-company) supports electronic complaints against insurers and asks for details including any previous complaint reference and supporting documents. Check its current scope and instructions for your issue. [CHI also publishes an insurance-complaint service](https://www.chi.gov.sa/en/servicesdirectory/pages/complaintagaisticfic.aspx); confirm the correct route for the health-insurance matter rather than assuming every insurance issue belongs to the same portal.

Keep the insurer’s decision and your requested resolution consistent across submissions. Do not assume filing a complaint guarantees payment or suspends every relevant deadline; check the applicable process and policy terms.

## Prepare for the next renewal

Use what you learned to review coverage, exclusions and service access before renewal. A lower premium alone does not explain how a policy will work for your needs. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a rejection mean there is no review route?**
Ask for the reason and the available review process before drawing that conclusion.

**Should I send my full medical history with a complaint?**
Provide the relevant information requested through the proper secure channel, not unnecessary sensitive records.

**Will a complaint guarantee reimbursement?**
No. The outcome depends on the policy, evidence and applicable process.
