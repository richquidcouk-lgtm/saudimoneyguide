## What's identical either way

Not everything differs — several major areas apply the same regardless of nationality:

- **No personal income tax** on employment salaries, for anyone.
- **VAT** (15%) applies identically to everyone's purchases.
- **Zakat** obligations follow the same religious/financial rules for those to whom they apply, independent of nationality.
- **Islamic finance structures** (Murabaha, Tawarruq) work the same mechanically for everyone using them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it generally cheaper or more expensive to be an expat financially in Saudi Arabia?**
Neither, structurally — it's different, not simply better or worse. No GOSI pension is a real long-term difference, but it's offset by no employee-side GOSI deduction reducing take-home pay in the meantime; which nets out better depends entirely on your own plans and how long you'll stay.

**Does this comparison apply the same way to every non-Saudi resident regardless of home country?**
Yes, for these structural rules — they're set by Saudi regulation based on Saudi/non-Saudi status, not by which country you're from. Bilateral agreements can occasionally affect specific narrow areas, which is worth checking for your specific situation if relevant.

**Which of these differences matters most for someone just arriving?**
Banking and credit-file building, practically speaking — it's the first thing you'll need, and starting it correctly (see our [first 90 days timeline](/blog/first-90-days-financial-checklist-saudi-arabia)) shapes how smoothly everything after it goes.

## Summary

Retirement, banking access, home finance eligibility, and health insurance arrangements are where nationality genuinely changes your financial situation in Saudi Arabia.
