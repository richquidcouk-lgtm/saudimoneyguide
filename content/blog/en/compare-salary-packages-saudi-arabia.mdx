A SAR 15,000 offer is not automatically better than a SAR 13,000 offer. One may include housing while the other expects you to fund rent, transport and annual travel from the headline amount. Compare each package on the same basis before negotiating.

## Start with the written offer

Separate basic salary, fixed cash allowances, benefits provided directly and variable compensation. [HRSD’s employer-and-worker guide](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-12/awareness-guide-on-the-rights-and-duties-of-employers-and-workers---1-.pdf) identifies agreed wages, benefits and allowances among the employment-contract details. Check the documented contract against the offer rather than relying on a recruiter’s verbal summary.

## An annual comparison you can copy

These are fictional packages, not Saudi market salary benchmarks.

| Item | Offer A | Offer B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Monthly basic pay | SAR 10,000 | SAR 11,000 |
| Monthly housing allowance | SAR 2,500 | SAR 0 |
| Monthly transport allowance | SAR 500 | SAR 500 |
| Guaranteed annual cash | SAR 156,000 | SAR 138,000 |
| Employer-provided accommodation | None | Included, details to verify |

Offer A provides SAR 18,000 more annual cash. If suitable accommodation under Offer B would otherwise cost you SAR 24,000 a year, it may offset that difference. But do not assign that value before checking the location, suitability for dependants, bills and conditions for keeping the accommodation.

## Cash flow matters as much as annual value

Ask whether a housing allowance is paid monthly, annually or after submitting evidence. An allowance paid later may not solve the cash requirement for a rent payment now. Prepare a first-three-month budget including moving costs and any costs you expect to recover later.

Run the cash components through the [salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator), using the employee contribution assumptions applicable to you. Keep a separate list of non-cash benefits: do not enter the estimated value of an employer-provided apartment as money paid into your account.

## Treat variable pay separately

Show a base case with no discretionary bonus and a second case with a clearly stated bonus assumption. Ask about performance conditions, first-year eligibility and payment timing. Use the base case for recurring commitments such as rent and loan payments; an uncertain bonus is a weak foundation for fixed bills.

Compare working hours, commuting costs, insurance details and family needs alongside the financial worksheet. A longer commute can absorb both the transport allowance and valuable time. Our [cost-of-living guide](/guides/cost-of-living-saudi-arabia) helps organize the expense categories; use actual quotes for your city and household.

## Questions to resolve before accepting

- Which amounts are guaranteed in the documented contract?
- Is housing suitable for my household, or only shared accommodation?
- Are benefits capped, reimbursed or supplied directly?
- When does the first payment arrive?
- What happens to benefits during probation or when employment ends?

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I compare offers by basic salary alone?**
No. Compare basic pay, fixed allowances, benefits and likely out-of-pocket costs separately.

**Can I add the maximum bonus to my normal salary?**
Keep it in an upside scenario unless it is genuinely guaranteed under the applicable terms.

**Is a bigger allowance always better?**
Its value depends on payment timing, eligibility and the cost it needs to cover.
