A car advertised with an affordable installment may leave a large amount due at the end. That final lump sum is often called a balloon payment. Understand how it fits into the agreement before deciding whether the monthly figure works for you.

## Follow every payment, not just the installment

Start with the cash price, down payment, financed amount, number of installments and final payment. Then record additional costs and whether they are already included. [SAMA’s consumer-finance disclosures](https://www.rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/entiresection/5508) cover key information such as the amount financed, installments and total payable. Obtain the documents for your specific product; do not assume all lease or ownership-transfer arrangements operate identically.

## A fictional offer with a balloon

| Component | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Upfront payment | SAR 10,000 |
| 60 monthly installments of SAR 1,200 | SAR 72,000 |
| Final payment | SAR 25,000 |
| Total of these payments | SAR 107,000 |

This illustration excludes any separate costs and is not an offer from a provider. If you want to build the SAR 25,000 final payment over 60 months without assuming investment returns, you need to set aside about SAR 416.67 monthly. Your installment plus balloon saving is therefore about SAR 1,616.67, before insurance and running costs.

## Why refinancing is not a complete plan

You might intend to refinance the balloon or sell the car, but future financing approval and resale value are uncertain. Test what happens if the vehicle sells for less than the amount due or if your income changes. Ask the provider about the actual end-of-contract options and document the conditions.

Do not assume that returning the vehicle automatically cancels every amount owed. The agreement’s condition, mileage, damage and settlement provisions may matter. Review those clauses before you treat a return option as equivalent to ownership.

## Add costs that affect the household budget

List insurance, fuel, maintenance, parking and other expected outgoings separately. Where a quoted payment already includes an item, avoid adding it again. Compare similar cars and the same period of use so the worksheet does not favor a cheaper-looking but less suitable option.

## Questions before signing

- Is the last payment additional to the final normal installment?
- Which charges and insurance amounts are included?
- What must happen to transfer ownership?
- What are the documented options if I cannot pay the balloon?
- What is the settlement process if I want to exit earlier?

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the lowest installment mean the cheapest car finance?**
No. Compare all payments and any costs outside them.

**Should I invest the balloon savings?**
Consider the fixed payment date and the risk of losing value or access when the money is needed.

**Is the final payment optional?**
Only the specific agreement can establish your options and obligations.
